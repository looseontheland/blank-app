The signal cut

Signal is the default view: 2,202 of the 3,443 bookmarks. Health & Body keeps everything readable — that shelf was already dense. The other 2,323 posts were read one at a time and kept only if they carried something reusable: a procedure, a named tool and how it fits, a copyable prompt or template, a teardown of why something works, a case study with numbers and method, non-obvious domain knowledge, or a real curated list. Cut: personal updates and flexes, motivation with no method, promos and withheld-how teasers, context-free replies, memes, opinions with nothing behind them.

The cut falls hardest where you'd expect. Mind & Self drops from 416 to 113 and Culture & Misc from 335 to 72; Business & Money keeps 542 of 727 and AI & Tech 401 of 591. Everything without a readable body is out of Signal by definition. Everything restores the full library, unchanged.

How this was built

3,136 of the 3,443 bookmarks were filed by reading the post's own text and assigning it one shelf, one sub-shelf, and a handful of tags.

The rest have no readable text. 73 were placed by looking at where the rest of that author's bookmarks landed — those are marked inferred and are a guess. The remaining 234 sit under Unsorted: 147 are long-form X Articles whose bodies the export never captured, 80 carry their content in an image or video, and 7 are too short to read.

Two limits worth knowing. The export left every author's screen name empty — only numeric ids survived, so authors can be clustered but not named. And the images could not be read: the machine that built this page is blocked from pbs.twimg.com, so the planned vision pass never ran.
